# Use Cases

Representative RBC workflows — who does what, and why it matters. The actor throughout is the ICP from `business/personas.md`: a solopreneur-developer shipping with an AI agent as their main collaborator.

## 1. First session in a fresh repo

The founder installs the skill and asks for landing page copy. The agent notices `/context/` doesn't exist, scaffolds it, and offers the three onboarding paths. They pick quick onboarding: 7 questions, about 5 minutes, 7 context files drafted — and the landing page copy that follows already uses their voice, personas, and value prop.

## 2. Marketing copy without re-explaining

"Write the launch post for LinkedIn." The agent loads `value-prop.md`, `personas.md`, `voice.md`, and `channels.md`, then writes copy that sounds like the founder, targets the actual ICP, and fits the channel. No pasted briefs, no "our tone is…" preamble.

## 3. The contradiction catch

Early on, the founder positioned the product for entry-level professionals. Weeks later, mid-conversation, they price the starter plan at $10k/month. Before writing anything, the agent checks `revenue-model.md` against its companion files and stops: "That pricing implies enterprise buyers, but `personas.md` says entry-level professionals. Rethink the pricing, or the positioning?" The founder decides; the agent updates every affected file in one pass. Decision assisted, update autonomous.

## 4. Scope-creep pushback

"Let's add team workspaces." The agent reads `strategy.md` and `personas.md`, sees the product is scoped solo-first, and pushes back before building: "This serves your secondary persona at best — build it anyway, or park it?" The idea lands in `backlog.md` instead of the codebase.

## 5. Context survives the gap

A feature ships; the agent updates `features.md` and announces it. Three months later, a new session starts — the agent reads `/context/` and picks up where the business actually is, not where it was at onboarding.
